Vibrations & thermochemistry

Vibrational density of states by element

g(ω) = Σk δ(ω − ωk) over the 441 harmonic modes, broadened by a 5 cm−1 Gaussian. Each element curve weights mode k by Σi∈X |eik|², the share of its mass-weighted amplitude on element X; the curves add up to the total.

Vibrational density of states by site

The same modes projected on core, surface and ligand atoms. Soft surface and ligand modes dominate the low-frequency range; the highest band shows which sites are stiffest.

Vibrational energies

ZPE = ½ Σk ħωk; Uvib(T) = ZPE + Σk ħωk / (eħωk/kBT − 1); Fvib = Uvib − T Svib. Both start at ZPE (dotted) at T → 0; their gap is the entropic term T Svib, which lowers free energies as T rises.

Vibrational entropy and heat capacity

With xk = ħωk/kBT: Svib = kB Σk [xk/(exk − 1) − ln(1 − e−xk)] and Cv = kB Σk xk² exk / (exk − 1)². Cv saturates at the classical (3N − 6) kB = 38.00 meV/K (dotted) once kBT exceeds the highest modes; Svib keeps growing, mostly from the soft modes.

Stability & ligands

Decomposition free energy

ΔGdec = G[(CdSe)55(CdCl2)13] − 55 G[CdSe, bulk] − 13 G[CdCl2, 1 M], per CdSe unit (solid; dotted: the electronic ΔEdec). It is the excess (surface) free energy of the dot against bulk CdSe with the ligands in solution: positive means metastable against ripening into the bulk, and it decreases as the dot grows.

Binding free energy

ΔGbind = (G[(CdSe)55(CdCl2)13] − 55 G[CdSe, 1 M] − 13 G[CdCl2, 1 M]) / 68, per unit (solid; dotted: electronic ΔEbind): the free energy of assembling the dot from monomers. More negative is more strongly bound; it rises with T because the free monomers gain translational and rotational entropy.

Stepwise CdCl2 desorption

ΔEk = E(dotk) + E(CdCl2) − E(dotk−1), where dotk is the k-th structure of the lowest path found (bars, MACE-relaxed). ΔGk(300 K) adds the vibrational change and the translational and rotational entropy of the released CdCl2 at 1 M (circles). Grey dots at k = 1: every symmetry-distinct surface site. Positive: bound. Beam search (width 2): units re-enumerated on the relaxed surface after every removal; units within 11 Å of the last removal relaxed, the others carried over and relaxed before being chosen (486 MACE relaxations).

Equilibrium ligand shell

Mean number ⟨k⟩ of CdCl2 units lost in equilibrium with CdCl2 in solution at μ = μ°(T) + Δμ, Δμ = kBT ln(c / 1 M): a Boltzmann average over all configurations evaluated by the search, each weighted by its symmetry, so it includes configurational entropy. Right: concentrated solution, shell intact; left: dilute, units desorb. At 300 K half of the units are lost at Δμ = -0.85 eV, i.e. c ≈ 10-14 M.
References computed with the same MACE head: bulk CdSe (cell-relaxed, phonons) and the CdSe and CdCl2 monomers as ideal-gas solutes at 1 M (harmonic vibrations, rigid-rotor rotation; the dot is treated the same way). Solvation and L-type binding of the released CdCl2 are not included, so detachment free energies are upper bounds.

Decomposition free energy in solution

ΔGdec = [Gsol(dot) − 55 G(CdSe, bulk) − 13 μ(CdCl2)] / 55, per CdSe unit, at the chosen solvent and [CdCl2]. Positive: the dot is metastable against ripening into bulk CdSe with the ligands dissolved; more CdCl2 in solution (higher c) stabilises the dot. Dotted: gas phase at 1 M.

Binding free energy and dissolution temperature

ΔGbind = [Gsol(dot) − 55 μ(CdSe) − 13 μ(CdCl2)] / 68, per unit. The dot assembles where it is negative and dissolves into monomers above Tdiss, where ΔGbind = 0: the translational entropy of the free monomers, kBT ln c, overcomes the bond energy. Lower monomer concentrations lower Tdiss. The precursor shift lowers the free energy of the monomer to mimic a stabilised molecular precursor (e.g. a Cd carboxylate / phosphine selenide pair) instead of the bare CdSe molecule.
